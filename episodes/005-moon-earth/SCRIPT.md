## Facts (with sources)

| # | Claim | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Moon is about 384,400 km away, moving away from Earth at ~3.8 cm per year | NASA, *Moon Facts*; McDonald et al. 2025 (Laser Ranging) |
| 2 | The drift is caused by tidal friction: Earth's rotation drags the ocean bulge ahead of the Moon, and that bulge pulls the Moon forward | NASA, *Tides*; Wikipedia, *Lunar distance* |
| 3 | The Moon slows Earth's spin as it drifts away: a day was ~22 hours 1.4 billion years ago | NASA/GSFC; Williams, *Geological constraints on the Precambrian history of Earth's rotation* (2000) |
| 4 | The Moon steadies Earth's axial tilt; without it the tilt could wobble by tens of degrees over long periods | Laskar, Joutel & Robutel 1993, *Nature*; NASA, *Earth's Moon* |
| 5 | The Moon raises the tides; tidal range on open coast is ~1 m, up to ~16 m in the Bay of Fundy | NOAA, *Tides and Water Levels*; Wikipedia, *Bay of Fundy* |
| 6 | The Moon is receding, not escaping: at the current rate it stays in orbit for billions of years | NASA; Wikipedia, *Lunar distance* |

**Physical cause and effect:** the Moon pulls the sea. The sea, dragged by a spinning
Earth, pulls the Moon. The Moon gains energy, rises, and Earth's day lengthens.

**Everyday analogy:** a figure skater pulling in her arms spins faster; opening them
slows her down. Earth is the skater. The Moon is her outstretched hand.

## Script (target ~60 s at ~3.7 words/s ≈ 225 words)

**Hook**
"The Moon is leaving. Not tonight. But it is leaving."

**Setting**
"Every year, it drifts about four centimetres further from Earth. About as fast as your fingernails grow."

**Action (escalation)**
"Over millions of years, that adds up. A day used to be twenty-two hours. The Moon used to fill more of the sky."

**Snap**
"And here is the strange part. The Moon is not drifting on its own. We are pushing it away."

**Deadpan double line**
"Luckily, it has been leaving for four billion years. Unluckily, it is taking the day with it."

**Explanation (analogy)**
"Picture a skater. Arms out, she spins slowly. Pull them in, she speeds up. Earth is the skater. The Moon is her outstretched hand."

**Consequence**
"The Moon tugs on our oceans. Those bulging seas tug back, and every tug hands the Moon a little more speed. Higher it climbs. Longer our day gets."

**What we lose**
"No Moon means smaller tides, and a tilt that could wobble. The seasons would stop behaving."

**Callback twist**
"Four centimetres a year. Your whole lifetime, about three metres. You will never see the difference. The Moon will."
